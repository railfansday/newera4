#3. Collision of two trains in Egypt kills at least seven

President orders prompt investigation after one train derails in collision in al-Ayyat about 50km from Cairo

Seven people were killed and at least 125 injured when two trains collided on Sunday south of the Egyptian capital, Cairo, officials said.

The crash occurred in al-Ayyat district in Giza province, about 50km (30 miles) south of Cairo, when a train travelling to Fayoum province crashed into the rear of a train heading to the southern province of Aswan, according to the transport ministry.

An inspection indicated that the Cairo-Aswan train had been stationary on the same track, officials said.

The health ministry gave the casualty toll, adding more than 70 of the injured were discharged after receiving treatment.

Train derailments and crashes are common in Egypt, where the railway system has a history of mismanagement and poor maintenance of equipment. In recent years the government has announced modernisation initiatives to improve its railways.

Ayman Gamal, a resident of Kafr Girza in al-Ayyat where the crash occurred, said people rushed to the scene, pulled out survivors and called ambulances.

Gamal said he saw about 80 of the injured being taken by ambulances to hospital. Some passengers were trapped inside one of the carriages of the stationary train and died, he said.

Egypt’s president, Abdel Fatah al-Sisi, said in a social media post that he had ordered a prompt investigation.

Outside al-Wasta central hospital in Beni Suef province, where some of the injured were taken for treatment, survivors said passengers were in shock and struggling to breathe because of all the dust that was kicked up. Nearby villagers eventually got them out, they said.

Farha Gamal, a survivor who was inside the Fayyoum train, said she saw some of the passengers who appeared dead and others were struggling to find their way out of the car. “There were other cars that were completely mangled,” she said in an interview.

Emergency teams and cranes were dispatched to the crash site to clear the wreckage and restore train services to their normal schedule as soon as possible, the Egyptian railway authority said.

Last September, a train driver and his assistant were injured after two freight trains collided in the same district.

Egypt’s deadliest train crash was in 2002 when more than 300 people were killed after a fire broke out on an overnight train journeying from Cairo to southern Egypt.

#2. Lewis Hamilton visits Egypt’s Rafah crossing and Gaza aid facilities

Seven-time F1 world champion tours border area and logistics warehouses operated by Egyptian Red Crescent

Lewis Hamilton has visited Egypt’s Rafah border crossing with the Gaza Strip to meet organisations sending humanitarian aid to the territory.

The seven-time Formula One world champion has spoken out about conflict and humanitarian crises in the region, and has called for peace. He has frequently posted on social media about children killed in Israeli airstrikes in Gaza and called for a ceasefire.

“I think we need to make sure we’re pushing to free Congo, free Sudan, free Syria, free Palestine. I think there’s a lot of peace that I’m hoping we get to at some stage,” Hamilton said in October.

A ceasefire agreement reached last year has halted major combat operations in Gaza, but the amount of aid reaching the territory continues to fall far short of the agreed-upon minimum of 600 trucks a day.

Israeli forces have continued to advance, expanding their control from 53% of the territory to at least 60%, an independent commission of UN experts said last month.

Airstrikes also continue, and Israeli fire along the advancing front has killed at least 196 Palestinians, including 18 women, 32 boys and 11 girls, the commission said.

Hamilton toured Egypt’s Rafah border crossing with Gaza before visiting logistics warehouses operated by the Egyptian Red Crescent, which facilitates the entry of aid to Gaza.

At the crossing, Hamilton greeted aid workers with “Assalamu alaikum” a widely used Arabic greeting meaning “peace be upon you.” He shook hands and briefly spoke to workers and officials.

Khaled Megawer, the governor of North Sinai, where Rafah is located, said the visit was intended to help Hamilton learn about Egypt’s efforts to send aid to Gaza.

The visit comes after Hamilton made his 400th race start on Sunday at the São Paulo Grand Prix at Interlagos.

The first black F1 driver, he made his debut with McLaren in 2007. He is only the second F1 driver to start 400 races after his former teammate Fernando Alonso, who achieved the milestone in 2024.

#1. Zimbabwean author Simbarashe Kundizeza cleared of using AI to write prize-winning story

After a month-long investigation it was concluded that Kundizeza’s story The Blue Fighter, which won the £10,000 Caine prize, was ‘human-written’

The Zimbabwean author Simbarashe Kundizeza has been cleared of using AI to write a prize-winning short story, after the prestigious Caine prize for African writers concluded that his entry was “human-written” after a month-long investigation.

Kundizeza’s story The Blue Fighter, which charts the declining fortunes of a family after the father invests in fish tanks, was announced as the winner of the £10,000 prize on 6 October.

Five days later, the Atlantic published an article titled “Yet another literary prize winner seems to be AI”, in which the author and journalist Vauhini Vara said the AI-detection software Pangram, which itself uses AI, had deemed the story to be almost completely artificially generated.

The Caine prize chair, Ellah Wakatama, said in a statement: “From our detailed investigation, we feel the evidence is clear: The Blue Fighter was human-written. We now look forward to awarding and celebrating Simbarashe Steyn Kundizeza … before the end of the year.”

She said: “When concerns were raised, we have a duty as an organisation to look at them carefully and fairly,” adding that the prize had taken a “human-centred” approach to its investigation.

“Our review included interviews with the author, the 2026 judging panel and The Shallow Tales Review, where the story was first published,” Wakatama said. “It also included independent and expert-led metadata analysis of drafts written by the author.”

Kundizeza, who had strongly denied using generative AI to write the story, said he was “relieved” that the allegations had been dismissed after the investigation. “It was not necessary in the first place, I am happy to put all this behind me and continue with my writing journey,” he said.

Vara’s article prompted outrage online, with many claiming that Black writers, and Africans in particular, were being unfairly singled out by allegations of the use of AI.

Associated Press contributed to this report
