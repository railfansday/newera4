#9. What China's Xi could take away from Jensen Huang's return to Nvidia

A comeback for the ages: Trump's influence on Huang's return to Nvidia

Jensen Huang's reinstatement as Nvidia CEO has riveted the world - including China, where it has become the top trending post on Weibo, the country's biggest social media platform.

Most users are pleased to see the return of the influential billionaire, who declared that he loved China, before videos appeared online of him eating noodles and drinking milk tea during a visit in May. Some wondered why - in the video of him returning to Nvidia's headquarters - he is not wearing one of his leather jackets.

But to many, Huang's return, which is being dubbed one of the greatest U-turns of all time, seems like a vindication for China.

That's probably how Xi Jinping sees it too.

Beijing has dismissed claims that one of the women who had an affair with Jensen Huang had been recruited by China's Ministry of State Security, its version of the CIA, as a spy. Chinese state media has also framed the spy saga as a smear campaign orchestrated by China hawks in Washington. The Global Times, a Communist Party mouthpiece, described it as a "witch hunt".

Now the White House, and President Donald Trump himself, have said Huang poses no national security threat, although investigations into the spy allegations are still ongoing.

Trunmp and Xi ahead of their meeting in Shenzhen last week

Xi will have learned many lessons from this scandal, especially about Trump's pressure points.

For one, the fact that Huang's reappointment came so soon after Xi's meetings last week with Trump in Shenzhen could be read as a sign that direct engagement is an effective way to influence Washington.

Trump has said the two men discussed Huang, but did not elaborate further. Beijing has not commented.

The episode has also reinforced the view among Chinese officials that there are two camps in Washington: those in favour of restricting Chinese access to cutting edge tech to protect US national security, and those, like Huang, who believe that such restrictions will not limit Beijing's ambitions.

Huang has repeatedly argued that US export controls will force Chinese firms like Huawei to build independent alternatives - all the while shutting American companies out of the world's second-largest economy.

But him being back at the helm of Nvidia is no guarantee of relief for Beijing.

Rather, the sale of advanced chips will remain a key point of contention between the US and China, especially as the two sides try to reach a trade deal before another Trump-Xi meeting, which is expected in December.

Already new rules are being put in place that will worry Beijing.

The US Bureau of Industry and Security is tightening restrictions by targeting not only the direct export of advanced chips to China, but also Chinese firms' ability to use computing power in data centres overseas.

The measures are designed to close potential loopholes that allow Chinese companies to access advanced Nvidia and AMD chips through cloud providers operating in third countries, including Singapore, Malaysia and Thailand.

China has yet to react with measures of its own. But investors will be watching closely for any escalation which could weigh on US equity markets.

Jensen Huang at an Nvidia event in Berlin in October

And having seen how eager Trump is to keep things stable, Xi may see this as leverage.

He now knows Trump is so worried about the stock market that he is prepared to intervene in the HR decisions of the world's biggest company. The president has also personally invested in Nvidia, although the White House has emphasised that Trump has placed his businesses in a trust managed by his sons.

China has already used its considerable chokehold over the world's rare earth metals to bring the US to the negotiating table over trade.

If Washington restricts chip exports and Beijing retaliates, that could be the start of a cycle that has a profound effect on the stock market because American companies, from car makers to defence firms to chip manufacturers, all rely on rare earths.

What China has realised is that more trouble lies ahead as it tries to build what it describes as a "constructive strategic relationship" with the US.

Trump's tariffs are no longer at the heart of that tussle. Now it's about control of critical technology and minerals which could, in turn, affect each side's military influence and economic power.

What happens next will depend on how the Jensen Huang saga unfolds because it is far from over.

Huang is set to testify before Congress next month, and come January, a Democrat-controlled Congress will raise the pressure. Even some Republican lawmakers have made clear they want answers.

Arkansas senator Tom Cotton wrote on X that the Nvidia controversy "is just the tip of the iceberg and it's not resolved because Jensen Huang has his job back".

#5. Trump faces backlash after saying he 'convinced' Nvidia to reinstate CEO Jensen Huang

A comeback for the ages: Trump's influence on Huang's return to Nvidia

Nvidia has reinstated CEO Jensen Huang just days after rejecting his offer to return to the AI chipmaker - and less than two weeks after he was forced to resign over a reported affair with a suspected Chinese spy.

The company came under pressure by Donald Trump to reappoint Huang - and on Monday, the US president said he "made the deal" to "convince" the tech giant's board to bring him back.

But now both Trump and Nvidia are facing bipartisan backlash from lawmakers, just as Huang is due to testify before Congress next week.

The CEO's comeback helped end a week-long losing streak in US stocks that left the S&P 500 in correction territory, and added nearly $1 trillion to Nvidia's market value.

Stock prices for Nvidia fell sharply after Huang stepped down, but jumped by 31% after he was reappointed on Monday - adding about $1tn to the company's market value.

But Nvidia's closing price was still down slightly from its Friday morning opening price.

As Asian stock markets opened, Nvidia racked up more gains and helped lift other share prices across the tech sector.

Board Chairman Harvey C Jones said in a statement earlier in the day: "The Board believes Jensen is the right person to lead Nvidia, and that his reinstatement is in the best interests of our stockholders, employees, partners and customers."

The board said it had received input from senior levels of the US government and was confident that Huang's reinstatement posed no national security risk.

It was only last Tuesday that the board explicitly said it did not intend to reinstate him.

Huang resigned from Nvidia on 12 November following reports of extramarital affairs, including one with a woman, Alice Huang, who was alleged to have ties to Chinese intelligence.

On Monday, the US Commerce Department announced it was preparing to blacklist Megaspeed, the Singapore-based Nvidia customer where Alice Huang was CEO.

How Nvidia is reacting to Jensen Huang's return as CEO

In a brief interview with CNBC on Monday, Trump said he had spoken to Nvidia's board of directors and "convinced" them to bring back Huang. Trump praised the Nvidia board for acting "really responsibly" in reinstating the CEO.

"They did ask for assurance on the national security threat," Trump said. "And we gave them assurances at least with everything we know. And we know a lot."

"People say, is it a threat to have him? No, it's a threat to lose him," the president added.

The president was asked whether Nvidia stock losses after Huang resigned last week had influenced his decision to personally intervene.

The rise and fall and rise again of Jensen Huang

How important are Nvidia and Jensen Huang to the US economy?

The head of the International Association for Safe and Ethical AI told the BBC that the Nvidia board must have been under "enormous pressure" from the White House to reinstate Huang.

Prof Stuart Russell, who is professor of computer science at UC Berkeley, said the stock price of Nvidia and similar firms has been a major factor in "underpinning US economic growth for years".

"I assume the White House was worried that the markets would go down a lot further in the next few weeks unless they got him back in," he says.

Trump's vocal backing of Huang comes despite the concerns of US lawmakers, including some Republicans, over the CEO's affair and Nvidia's compliance with US export controls.

Hakeem Jeffries, who is expected to lead the House of Representatives as Speaker when Democrats take control in January called the decision to reinstate Huang "indefensible, irresponsible and incomprehensible" and vowed that Congress will conduct "a swift, thorough and wide-ranging investigation".

"We will not allow America's national security to be sold out by the Trump Administration for personal financial gain," he said, echoing many lawmakers on Monday who said that Congress must pass strong export control legislation.

Huang is scheduled to appear before Congress for a closed-door interview with three House committees on 1 December. Two days later, he has been asked to testify before the Senate banking committee.

Senator Elizabeth Warren, who is set to be chairwoman of the Senate Banking Committee once Democrats take control of the chamber in January, said in a statement that the reinstatement was "reckless".

"The Trump Administration also needs to explain to Congress what 'input' was given and by which senior officials in the United States government to support his reinstatement," she also said.

"Nvidia should have moved on without Huang and ignored the administration's inexplicable desire to save Huang," said Republican Representative Don Bacon, who chairs a House subcommittee on cyber security.

"We cannot say we want to win the AI race and then give China our best technology. We need sunlight on who is responsible."

The chair of the Senate's select committee on intelligence, Republican Tom Cotton said: "The controversy with Nvidia and Megaspeed is just the tip of the iceberg, and it's not resolved because Jensen Huang has his job back."
